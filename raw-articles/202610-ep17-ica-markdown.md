### Kal'tsit·Esperanta

{{< mastery-table-enhanced id="1052_kalts2" rows="S2M3,S++,S++|S3M3,A-,S" >}}

Kal'tsit makes her extremely predictable return to Rhodes Island with an annoying non-standard character in her name that isn't on my keyboard and... is still a Medic that does True Damage. I'm not sure what I expected. In any case, her new form is incredibly powerful, albeit in a slightly subtle way. I say subtle because while AoE True Damage isn't exactly subtle itself, the total value of her kit including that True Damage, strong AoE healing, control, permanent Take-Off that makes her immune to most damage and mechanics, and an Advanced flex skill, come together in a very powerful and flexible package that tends to be even more than the sum of the parts. Especially considering Medics tend to be fairly rare, Kal'tsit dot Esperanta should be meta for a good long while.

As with many units with a lot of parts and flexibility, she has two key skills with differing usage. However, in Kal2’s case, one skill has a significantly more valuable Mastery, her S2. With it, she gains 10 shots, each of which deals a wide swath of AoE True Damage to enemies, and big healing to allies. Then add in a long 5 second Slow that will keep enemies in range snared for the entire duration, while permanently floating, and you have an extremely powerful all-in-one skill that can solve a *lot* of problems. There is some mechanical "fiddliness" to it due to her prioritization of enemies, but unlike the now ancient Folinic, the splash range is wide enough, and the impact per shot big enough, that an occasional misplaced shot doesn't really matter much.

Mastery on it is quite valuable as well since it double dips on increases to both damage and healing, which in turn both double dip off of two different ATK increases. The damage per shot increases by 30%, while the healing per shot increases by over 40%, which is a staggering total gain! Many units who have this level of gain can have Masteries that feel “essential”, but in Kal2’s case, the effect is already really good at SL7. That’s a pretty nice feature because it means Kal2 is still great in IS before her E2 promotion, or for just starting rosters! But S2M3 really amps the skill up to another level, and is thus among the highest priority Masteries.

Next, her S3 is one of the real "highlight" skills. It can pull off some really crazy stuff, and can be a key solution to a lot of really tough mechanics. She can entirely reposition herself while pulling back two allies to the bench to be redeployed for free. Since she has permanent Take-Off, this can be done at the start of certain dangerous thresholds, keep herself safe, then redeploy her allies when it's over. Or it can be used to reset long cooldowns. Or it can be used to rapidly adjust between phases. Or a number of others! It's really the kind of skill where the sky is the limit (get it, because she floats) in terms of creative play.

However, the weird thing with it is that her S3 Masteries aren't all that essential to that function. They do matter and do have an impact, but often not in a way that more casual Story centric players will care about, especially for an occasional use skill. Mastery represents a 15 SP improvement to her initial wind-up, a 10 SP cycle time improvement via reduced SP cost, plus some additional healing. The initial SP is probably the most notable part since it is a rather large amount, but it's not often a skill that's helidropped. The cycle time via SP cost is also quite strong, especially given the high base cost, but it's also rarely the kind of skill that gets activated immediately off cooldown. Finally, the healing on it is actually pretty strong, and can very much matter, especially in the high pressure situations the skill is used in, but isn't the key aspect of the skill. These are all things that absolutely will matter in min-maxed scenarios, but may not matter at all in casual gameplay. So whether her S3 Masteries are worth pursuing or not depends on the kind of player you are, and for many with limited resources, it may be a skip entirely, at least in the short-term.

Last, probably pass on her S1. The interval reduction on her S3 means that the healing difference between the skills is comparable, so the main difference comes down to SP cost and the extra block count vs aerial enemies. The former doesn't justify an expensive 6★ Mastery, especially when her S2 is a far better daily driver, and the latter, like so many similar skills, just isn't valuable with the current design of the game.

### Closure

{{< mastery-table-enhanced id="4228_closur" rows="S3M3,S+,A+|S2M3,S-,S+" >}}

At long last, Closure moves out of the procurement department where she puts us in real money debt, to field operations, where she puts us in DP debt instead. Some things never change. Fortunately for us, Closure makes for an extremely strong Vanguard. She has DPS that rivals some dedicated DPS units, the best DP generating potential available, albeit with some caveats, and the mentioned "DP debt" mechanic via her Talent 2 that proves to be surprisingly clutch. It makes Closure an incredibly strong option, even despite two meta-tier Vanguards already existing at her release. In fact, she will often be better than them, depending on the circumstances, and will tend to be easier to use as well.

In that vein of thinking, Closure's "best" skill will depend on what you want out of her. Some players will find her S3 to be the most valuable, while others will find that to be her S2! Starting with her S3, it leans a bit more towards being a "daily-driver" sort of skill. It's the skill that has the high DPS, which can be done on an absolutely massive range, in addition to generating solid DP. Story-centric players will likely find it to be the best Vanguard skill in the game due to these aspects, allowing Closure to perform triple duty as an early hold, a DP generator, and even a straight-up DPS unit. The new "Sluggish" is notable too, although at least as of this writing, hasn't found much meta use. Because of that, Advanced players may not find it as valuable, especially if you're already using the other 6★ Vanguards on your team. It's a great combination of abilities, but isn't unique or essential in high-meta situations, and the DPH on it is relatively low.

Mastery on her S3 is quite good as well. The most obvious improvement is to her DPS, which increases by around 33%, but the more notable one is to her initial SP. At SL7, she requires a painfully long 18 SP for initial activation, an eternity for a Vanguard, but that drops to just 7 SP at S3M3. Part of that is a 6 SP drop in base cost, which is also a nice improvement considering the DPS potential of the skill. The debuff improves as well, although as previously mentioned, that hasn't proven to be all that essential yet, with the main benefit being the MVSPD reduction thanks to her big range.

Meanwhile, her S2 will likely be the more appealing skill for Advanced players, although the Mastery gains on it are more muted. There are a lot of features to the skill, but the only one that really matters is the DP refund. With it, plus the native DP generation, she can effectively dump out an entire roster as soon as the skill is up. This makes it tremendously valuable in terms of effective DP generation, and she'll be a key part in many (if not all) CC-tier DP-constrained strategies because of it. However, that is restricted to the 3x3 square around her summon, so it's not quite universally great either, and it lacks her S3's damage which can often hold a map on its own.

As far as priority goes, while there are a lot of gains over Mastery on her S2, most of them don't especially matter. She does gain more damage, and more DEF, which also applies to allies within that 3x3 square, but those aren’t the main feature of the skill. Instead, the main value of Mastery is to the initial SP and the extra DP gained. The DP gain is actually subtly important since she needs a bit of a DP bank built up before she can really pour out the team, but the total gain is still less dramatic than her S3. Advanced players especially will better appreciate those gains on a skill like this, but Story centric players will find S3's Masteries more valuable.

Last, you should probably pass on her S1. There's two problems with it. First, while the DP generation can get very high, it's poor to start and takes some time to ramp up. By the time it does ramp up, it probably doesn't matter anymore. There aren't a lot of situations where her S1 is better than her S2! Second, the Auto Activation that probably makes it appealing to some makes the shield aspect unreliable, limiting its niche value. That leaves it as a desire-only tier of skill, to show off your love of capitalism, although it has had a few small highlights for the true min-maxers.

### Crackborne

{{< mastery-table-enhanced id="4225_tanya" rows="S2M3,B-,B" >}}

Crackborne can feel like a rather uninspired unit. She is extremely similar to the original Primal Protector, Philae, with only some minor tweaks to better align with Corrosion. However, that combination tends to be weaker than Philae, who is not a new unit anymore and is already not a common recommendation. Worse, she doesn't stack with Cairn, one of the best Defenders at the rarity, since the Elemental types are incompatible. This means she doesn't have much niche use either. However, while Corrosion's damage is much lower, as is Crackborne's bulk which quickly vanishes, she can cycle and trigger it faster than Philae can. It's hard to call 5,000 AoE true-ish damage plus a debuff on a 25 SP cycle bad!

If you do have a particular fondness for off-meta bears, then Crackborne's main skill is her S2. As with Philae, she reflects hits back as her injury type. But unlike Philae, she does this much faster. There's two notable things. First, the reflect has no cooldown timer, meaning it can trigger extremely fast depending on the enemy. This can be a huge boon for swarms, although the relatively low damage per injury burst holds this back. Second, the skill applies as a buff to herself, meaning it doesn't have a typical uptime and begins charging right away. This means she can trigger it as fast as every 25 seconds, although this is still dependent on the enemy.

As with all Elemental Injury dealers, there are a million different permutations that impact a multitude of trigger thresholds. This is made even more complex here due to the fact the injury increases at every Mastery level, meaning going through all of them is simply untenable, but Crackborne's Masteries do end up being relatively valuable. For example, Mastery is required to trigger Corrosion in a single skill activation (5 hits) if her Module condition can't be triggered. It's also required to reach 4 hits without also having max level. While there are many common scenarios where Mastery doesn't matter, the scenarios that do, plus some meaningful SP improvements, make it a Mastery worth pursuing if you plan to actually use her.

As for her S1, it's a very fringe consideration. Skills like this do have some niche value, but Philae's S1 is a better version of it. Their net effective Elemental healing is actually pretty similar, but Philae's version cycles faster, and it's already an extremely fringe tool. Considering that even in the rare circumstances that you did need such a skill, Philae is easily available from the shop, the only reason to really consider Crackborne's S1 is because you specifically like Crackborne more.

### Viy

{{< mastery-table-enhanced id="4226_veen" rows="S3M3,C,None|S2M3,None,None" >}}

Even by the standards of welfare 6★s, Viy is extremely uninspired. He's a Mystic Caster, who are already weak in the modern meta, but doesn't even do the thing Mystic Casters are good at all that well. A *four* year old unit who wasn't great when he was new either, Ebenholz, does it better. Viy instead trades the big single-target blast for chain damage. Then, as a 6★, he's also very expensive to promote. Other recent welfare 6★s have had some respectable niche value, but that isn't the case with Viy, meaning he's a unit to avoid raising at all. However, that said, he isn't actually bad enough to be completely ungraded by the standards of this guide. If he appeals to you, he does enough damage to be usable. Just keep in mind that he is entirely a desire-only promotion!

If that's you, his only skill worth much consideration is his S3, which has his best damage on it and gels with his kit better. Essentially, what he wants to do is trigger his Talent 2 on everything in range, which will make him save charges, and thus trigger the extra damage from his Talent 1. S3 accomplishes this much better than his other skills due to the bounce and extreme amount of ASPD, resulting in far better total damage. Mastery is fairly valuable too since it grants him a lot more ammo, in addition to dual increases to ASPD and ATK.

Meanwhile, his S2 will have better single-target damage, so may be worth considering for the true husbando maximalists, although it's relatively hard to use compared to Ebenholz (who also does more damage). If he can get all 9 of his charges up in advance, and you trigger the skill first, he can stack all of the buffs at once, resulting in some respectable damage. However, he has no way of managing those charges, so under any semblance of pressure, it just becomes a slow ramp-up DPS skill.

Last, his S1 is... a skill that exists. I'll admit I'm having a tough time finding things to say about it because it's uninspired filler on an uninspired unit. Amusingly, it’s actually his only skill with records on [arkrec](https://ironcarrotarchives.com/masteries/glossary/#arkrec), as of this writing, but to the point, it’s almost all within the E0 niche!
